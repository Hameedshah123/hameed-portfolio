How a case gets resolved

The same five-step sequence whether it's a stalled enterprise account or a 2 a.m. integration failure.

01

Diagnose

Read the system as it actually behaves, not as the documentation says it should. Logs and account data first, assumptions never.

02

Design

Sketch the fix as a data flow before writing a line of code — what moves where, what triggers what, what fails safely.

03

Build

Working code over a polished deck. Test against real account data, not a happy-path demo.

04

Automate

If I'd do it twice, it becomes a script. If it's a script, it becomes a workflow that runs without me.

05

Hand off

Document it so someone else can run it without me in the room. Knowledge transfer is part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.

About

The short version

Hameed ShahIslamabad, PK · Customer Success & AI Systems

I lead Customer Success and Onboarding at Payactiv, a B-Corp earned-wage-access platform, where I've spent five-plus years running a high-volume book of enterprise accounts — several with workforces of 10,000 to 20,000-plus employees — and the team behind them. The job is equal parts relationship and infrastructure — accounts don't renew because someone was nice on a call, they renew because onboarding worked, integrations held, and someone caught the failure before it became a headline.

That second half is what pulled me into building. I write the Python that replaces the manual queue, the n8n workflow that watches for a stalled account, the API bridge that goes up at 2 a.m. when a client's payroll system stops talking to ours. Most of it never ships as a product with my name on it — it just quietly keeps things running.

Outside of Payactiv, I keep building. I run an agent framework on Llama 3.1 and LangChain that powers a dental billing assistant, a WhatsApp concierge for a Hajj & Umrah travel business, and I'm teaching the same models to read the Pakistan Stock Exchange. On the purely creative side, I designed and independently published a fully AI-illustrated coloring book on Amazon, working across Nano Banana, ImagineArt, and Leonardo AI to keep the line art consistent page to page. I work US and Canada hours from Islamabad, which mostly just means I've gotten good at building bridges — between systems, and between time zones.
